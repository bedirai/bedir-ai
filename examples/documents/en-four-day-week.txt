MEMO TO THE CITY COUNCIL: PILOT RESULTS (synthetic sample)

From: Department of Human Resources. Subject: six-month four-day week pilot in the Permits and Licensing Office.

Design. From January to June 2026, 62 office staff worked 32 hours over four days at full pay. A comparison group of 58 staff in the Tax Office kept the five-day, 40-hour schedule.

Results
Sick leave in the pilot group fell from 6.1 to 4.0 days per person, annualised. In the comparison group it stayed at 5.8 days.
Median processing time for building permits fell from 21 to 18 working days.
Two of nine planned resignations in the pilot group were withdrawn. Staff satisfaction in the survey rose from 54% to 81%.
Average phone waiting time rose from 4 to 7 minutes, because the office is now closed on Fridays.
Overtime costs in the pilot group rose by 9%, mostly in March during the seasonal peak.
Citizen complaints about opening hours rose from 12 to 31 over the six months.

Cost. Extending the scheme to all 1,400 office staff would require about 60 new hires to keep Friday services open, at an estimated 3.1 million per year.

Limits. The pilot was not randomised. Permit applications were 7% lower than in the same period of 2025. The staff union has asked for the scheme to be made permanent before the budget vote.

Recommendation. The department recommends a second, randomised pilot in three more offices before a city-wide decision.
